Why AI Lie? - From Noise to Narrative: Tracing the Origins of Hallucinations in Transformers Summary

11 January 2026

Summary

As generative AI systems become widely adapted in the society, deeper insight into occasional volatility such as hallucinations poses an acute need, especially in high-stakes areas. The research paper "From Noise to Narrative: Tracing the Origins of Hallucinations in Transformers" by Suresh et al. (2025) explores this concept. The purpose of this study is to show when a transformer received ambiguous input, how and when hallucinations arise.

In the experiment, pre-trained transformer models in both vision(ViT) and language(Gemma-2B) is used, internal residual stream activations are extracted at multiple layers. Then, the authors train sparse autoencoders(SAEs) as probes on residual stream activations. SAEs trained from pure noise is employed to rule out the possibility that SAE features learned from semantic input data, as well as SAEs trained conventionally for smaller/larger models for comparison. The authors systematically degrade inputs by shuffling image patches adn text n-grams. The number of active SAE features across layers is measured. Finally, hallucination scores are measured using HHEM-2.1.

The results are:

  1. Semantic concepts emerge even with noise-trained SAEs.
  2. The number of active SAE features increases as input structure is degraded, peaking in middle layers.
  3. Hallucination likelihood can be predicted from SAE feature activations extracted before output generation.
  4. Suppressing hallucination-associated features reduces hallucinations.

The results above lead the authors to draw the following conclusions:

  1. Transformers activate semantic concepts in middle layers, reflecting model priors.
  2. Transformers impose meaning rather than passively extracting it.
  3. Hallucination can be predicted before output generation.

Analysis

The experiment used pure noised trained SAEs, which isolate the input and model priors. It also used gradual degradation of input structure to observe concept expansion. It covers both vision and language transformer models, which generalise the theory well. Further, Hallucination signals are found in middle layer representations, which shows hallucination is a representation level phenomenon.

There are also some limitations in this research paper. Noise trained SAEs do not rule out architectual priors, training object effects, only SAE training bias. Features are labeled via top activating examples and human inspection, which introduces interpretive bias. Hallucinations are evaluated singualy using a language model. SAE feature capture some machanisms, but not the full process.

Despect the limitations, the paper is still relevant and essential when our society are in the beginning of adapting AI into our day to day life. It shows hallucinatino risk is predictable before output generation. Hallucination arise mostly during middle layers. This make the prevent or reduce hallucination possible. It also demonstrates semantic concept appear even under pure noise, which explain why some transfomers is wrong confidently. It introduced new method that training SAEs on pure noise inputs. Finally, It generalizes across vision and language.

JorGPT: An system that use LLMs to automate grading - JorGPT: Instructor-Aided Grading of programming Assignments with Large Language Models (LLMs) Summary

30 January 2026

Summary

The authors address two main problems in manual grading: heavy instructor workload and grading inconsistency. In a typical large class, manual marking can exceed hundreds of hours per semester. JorGPT integrates multiple commercial LLMs into a Python-based interface connected to Canvas. Instructors can upload student submissions, customize rubrics, select models, and review AI-generated grades and feedback before releasing results.

The study evaluates 672 student submissions from a C programming course. Quantitative analysis shows strong alignment between AI and instructor grades. Several models achieved Pearson correlations above 0.9 with human grading. A regression-based ensemble model combining multiple LLM outputs achieved high predictive accuracy (adjusted R² = 0.9156, MAE ≈ 0.46 on a 0–10 scale), indicating that AI-generated grades can closely approximate instructor judgments. Validation results show that about 90% of predictions differed by 0.5 points or less, which the authors consider pedagogically acceptable.

Analysis

Qualitative analysis demonstrates that LLM feedback is generally clear, rubric-aligned, and pedagogically useful across high-, medium-, and low-quality submissions. However, limitations remain, including potential grading bias, difficulties handling unconventional solutions, and limited explainability.

Efficiency analysis shows substantial time and cost benefits. Automated grading reduced processing time from an estimated 300+ hours of manual work to minutes or hours, depending on the model. Costs per batch of submissions were low, with some models operating under free tiers and others costing only a few euros.

The authors conclude that LLMs are effective for semi-automated grading, but emphasize the importance of human-in-the-loop supervision. They recommend hybrid workflows where AI performs initial grading and feedback generation, while instructors retain final authority. Future work should focus on bias auditing, prompt optimization, adaptive feedback, and evaluating impacts on student learning outcomes.

Limitations:

  1. 1. Limited geralizability of the dataset
  2. 2. Dependence on human grading as ground truth
  3. 3. Prompt sensitivity not expored

My thoughts: While I don't agree using generative AI to grade human work because of the hallucination of ai and bias, We can learn from the method: The use of correlation analysis, regression modeling, cross-validation, MAE... it provides replicable frame work for reliability.
